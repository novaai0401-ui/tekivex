Two everyday conversion headaches: iPhone photos saved as HEIC that will not open on a Windows PC or in some apps, and needing to combine several photos into a single PDF for a form or upload. Both are easy to fix once you know what is going on. This guide explains the HEIC format and shows how to convert images to JPG or into a PDF, privately and for free.

Why iPhone photos are HEIC

By default, modern iPhones save photos in HEIC, a format that stores high quality in a smaller file than JPG. The catch is that HEIC is not universally supported: send one to a Windows PC or upload it to an older web form and it may refuse to open. Converting the file to JPG solves this instantly, because JPG opens everywhere.

Converting HEIC to JPG

Converting is simply re-saving the image in the JPG format. The visible quality stays effectively the same for normal use; you just get a file that every device and app understands. If you regularly send photos to non-Apple devices, converting to JPG before sharing avoids the "I can’t open this" reply.

Combining images into one PDF

Forms, applications and portals often ask for a single PDF rather than a pile of separate images. Combining photos into one PDF puts each image on its own page in the order you choose, producing one tidy file that is easy to upload and to read. It is the standard way to submit scanned documents, receipts or ID photos.
